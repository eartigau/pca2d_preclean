TOI-2120, the demonstration case

An M dwarf observed with SPIRou: 321 exposures over 452 days, a barycentric velocity sweeping 37.9 km/s, and deep water bands crossing the stellar lines. It is the case the method was built for, and it shows what the method can do when the geometry is favourable.

What the components turned out to be

With one stellar and three Earth components, the rank correlations against every quantity the headers carry are these. They are the run's own correlations.csv, not a separate analysis.

Three things are worth saying plainly about this table, because none of them is what one would have predicted:

  • Nothing follows the airmass. The largest airmass correlation anywhere in the table is 0.18, on the stellar component. For a block that is supposed to be capturing the atmosphere, that is not the expected signature, and it says the residual tellurics APERO left behind are no longer airmass-ordered.
  • \(b_3\) follows the clock at 0.805, and nothing else. A component that drifts monotonically over 452 days is an instrumental drift, not weather: this is the detector or the wavelength solution ageing, and the fit is removing it.
  • \(b_2\) follows the BERV at −0.737 while being flat in airmass. That is not a water column. It is the signature of something whose apparent amplitude depends on how far the stellar spectrum has slid past it: the residual of an imperfect separation between the two frames, which the fit absorbs into the observer block. It is doing useful work there, and it is also the component to watch, since a BERV-ordered term is exactly what one must not accidentally remove from a velocity.

The stellar component's strongest partner is the seeing, at −0.603, with the median flux at +0.395: \(a_1\) is largely tracking how much light went down the fibre, which is a line-shape effect rather than a photospheric one. On a star where activity mattered more, one would expect this row to look different.

Three HARPS campaigns, three ways

TOI-2120 is the favourable case. The harder test is the optical, where the tellurics have already been cleaned once and what is left is a residual of a residual. Each star below is measured three ways on the same exposures, in a single instrument mode, with the same frozen version of the code.

GJ 581's CCF row excludes three exposures where the DRS fails outright, returning −591 to −655 m/s where LBL finds −3 to +12; with those three the CCF rms is 97.8 m/s. pca2d-preclean is the only one of the three pipelines to beat the CCF on all three stars, and the gain is concentrated in the tails: it straightens the worst exposures more than it improves the median one, which is why the MAD moves less than the rms.

The gains in the optical are small, and that is the honest reading. A few percent on a 2 m/s star is not a breakthrough; it is a consistent improvement that holds across spectral type. The factor of three on TOI-2120 is what the method does when the contamination is large and the BERV span is wide. Both numbers are the method.

When it does not help

The separation of the two blocks rests entirely on the star moving relative to the sky. Take that away and the fit has no information to work with, and a fit with no information still returns an answer.

2× better
Same star, 0.7 km/s span
2.4× worse

Two slices of the same campaign, identical in every other respect. A campaign observed over a narrow range of barycentric velocity should not be precleaned this way, and the run's own report plots the BERV coverage on its first pages so that the question is asked before the answer is believed.

Other failure modes found by measurement. Two stellar components go degenerate on TOI-2120: the second amplitude starts tracking the sky, absorbs a 232 m/s shift, and the rms climbs to 67 m/s. A shared basis across several objects needs shared nights, not merely similar SNR: fitting GJ 1, Proxima and a third target together with only two nights in common made GJ 1 worse (3.04 against 2.61 solo).

How these numbers were produced

  1. One code version per comparison. Every star in a comparison runs on the same commit, frozen before the first run and recorded in the output. If a fix lands mid-campaign, everything is redone, even when the fix is provably cosmetic, because traceability is the point, not numerical equivalence.
  2. One instrument mode per star. The mode with the most epochs, never a mixture.
  3. Paired comparisons only. The same exposures on both sides, matched by timestamp; an epoch missing from either series is dropped from both.
  4. Robust statistics beside the plain ones. rms and MAD = 1.4826 × median absolute deviation, always both, because a correction that fixes the tails and a correction that fixes the core are different things.

Every run writes its resolved configuration, its component counts, its LBL version and a PDF report of around thirty pages beside its outputs, so any number on this page can be traced back to the run that produced it.
