\section*{The short answer}

\textbf{On the current code, Proxima and GJ~1 no longer lose.} Proxima gains
2\%{} (rms 2.86 to 2.80~m/s on the same 765 exposures, paired bootstrap
1.005--1.042), and GJ~1 is at break-even (0.98, not significant; 1.03 with the
best setting of the night). The losses quoted until yesterday (Proxima 2.86 to
3.34, later 3.04) were measured on September code. Three fixes since then
touch both stars: LBL measures the corrected spectra against its own template
(\texttt{lbl.star\_template: false}, 2026-09-12), deep line cores are no
longer clipped (\texttt{7b88be6}), and the high pass is taken against a
template (\texttt{c61b652}). LBL also loads the HE profile for these HE data
since \texttt{lbl-rapide 95bf4f4}. Everything below ran on one frozen
version, pca2d \texttt{ba36dce}.

What remains is the shape of the trade at high SNR: the correction removes a
real systematic, but one of about the same size as the noise it adds. Error
bars grow by 5--9\%{} on GJ~1 and 11--16\%{} on Proxima, and the intra-night
scatter by 0.3 to 0.8~m/s in quadrature, depending on the star and the
setting.

\section*{What was learned}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The template hypothesis (H1 of the plan) was already settled.}
  The plan leaned on a September measurement where the fit's star template cost
  Proxima 1~m/s. The configuration has not used that template since
  2026-09-12. The ``template matrix'' run tonight therefore compared LBL's
  template with itself and is identical to $10^{-4}$~m/s; it is reported as a
  check, not as a result.

\item \textbf{On a nightly-stacked cube, the residual cuts reach only the first
  exposure of each night.} The cube's row for a night carries the name of its
  first exposure, and the correct stage looks the cuts up by file name.
  Measured in the headers: GJ~1 first exposures lose a median of 2658 samples
  to the cuts, the others none (100\% at zero); Proxima 7790 against none
  (99\%). This is a bug, and it only touches stacked cubes, which on this
  project means NIRPS. TOI-2120 is not stacked and every exposure is cut.

\item \textbf{What the cuts do depends on the star.} Removed on GJ~1 they help
  everywhere (rms 2.71 to 2.61, intra-night 1.53 to 1.35); the cut first
  exposures took 1.88~m/s of added velocity against 1.55 for the others, and
  14\% more error against 6\%. Removed on Proxima they hurt the night-to-night
  scatter (2.88 to 2.96) and enlarge the $V_{\rm tot}$ bias (4.6 to
  6.8~$\sigma$) while helping the photon noise. The cuts therefore remove a
  real systematic on Proxima and cost more than they remove on GJ~1. With the
  bug in place the two cannot be told apart from a target dependence; fixing it
  is the next experiment.

\item \textbf{The no-weight mask is worth keeping.} With no NaN added at all,
  GJ~1's error bars and intra-night scatter drop below the delivered ones (1.50
  and 1.25 against 1.53 and 1.28) but the nightly scatter rises to 2.64: the
  samples the fit could not weight carry night-to-night systematics.

\item \textbf{The correction carries real information on a bright star.}
  Correcting each GJ~1 night with the amplitudes of another night (the null
  test) gives a gain of 0.94 (0.90--0.97) against 0.98 for the real
  amplitudes, and a nightly scatter of 2.64 against 2.49.

\item \textbf{The basis does not overfit.} GJ~1 cross-validated: the 70
  odd-ranked nights were held out of the fit and corrected by a basis that
  never saw them. On those nights the result matches the in-sample correction
  (cuts off on both sides): rms 2.58 against 2.61, nightly 2.42 against 2.45,
  error 1.58 against 1.57. The basis learns nothing from the noise of the
  nights it corrects, so the cost does not come from estimating it.

\item \textbf{The added intra-night noise is not the per-exposure amplitude
  refit.} Regressing the intra-night added velocity on each exposure's own
  amplitudes explains 4\% of its variance on GJ~1, 9\% on Proxima and 14\% on
  TOI-2120. Something else in the per-exposure path adds it.

\item \textbf{The faint control shows the same cost.} On TOI-2120 (run of
  2026-10-16) the correction gains a factor of 2.9 overall, yet the
  intra-night scatter rises from 7.76 to 10.63~m/s. The cost exists at every
  SNR; it only decides the outcome where the systematic is small.

\item \textbf{No stellar component helps GJ~1 a little.} With the cuts off,
  0-3 gives the best GJ~1 result of the night (rms 2.58, nightly 2.40 against
  2.50 delivered, gain 1.03, 0.996--1.065). Not measured on Proxima: the disk
  did not allow it.

\item \textbf{Not conclusive:} the telluric-support restriction at 0.995 keeps
  93.5\% of the near-infrared columns and behaves like the nominal. A lower
  threshold would be needed for a real test.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{What did not run, and why}

Proxima's \texttt{k0c} (no stellar component, cuts off), its cross-validation
and its no-NaN variant were skipped for lack of disk. The first Proxima cube
build filled the internal disk, most likely through the system's swap (22~GB
in use just after), and the run failed; the GJ~1 products of the night were then moved to irrisor,
links left in place, and Proxima was run again for the nominal and the
cuts-off variant only. The planet injection was dropped before the night for
the same reason. A full corrected set of Proxima is 11.5~GB and its LBL
products 3.5~GB per object.

\noindent One more incident: the queue file's last line had no newline, and
the runner's \texttt{read} loop skips such a line, so the LBL step of GJ~1's
cross-validation (G09L) was run by hand after the queue ended. It took three
hours instead of seven minutes: the runner's \texttt{caffeinate} ended with
the runner, and the machine slept, advancing the job only in short wakes. The
result is unaffected.

\section*{Proposed next steps}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Fix the stacked-cube cuts so that every exposure of a night gets its
  night's cuts, and measure GJ~1 and Proxima again (nominal and cuts off).
\item Find the per-exposure source of the intra-night noise (neither the
  amplitude refit nor the basis estimate): candidates are the per-exposure
  no-weight mask, the high pass of each file, and the interpolation back onto
  the native grid.
\item Rerun TOI-1452 and GJ~328 on the frozen code, so that the
  gain-versus-SNR figure of the talk rests on one version.
\item Free disk space before any further Proxima night (cubes in
  \texttt{cache/}, 37~GB; old corrected sets on irrisor).
\end{enumerate}
